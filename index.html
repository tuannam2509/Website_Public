Greetings!

I am an Associate Professor of Economics at Miami University. I hold a PhD in Economics from Vanderbilt University. My fields of specialization are international finance, macroeconomics, and applied time-series econometrics. I am also an Associate Editor for Economic Inquiry and International Finance.

Research

Publications, working papers, and ongoing projects.

Publications

  1. "The Real Effects of Brexit on Labor Demand: Evidence from Firm-level Data." (2025).
    Journal of International Economics with Hang Do, Kiet Duong and Toan Huynh.
    Summary: We document how the Brexit shock affected the demand for labor at the individual firm level, shedding light on employment friction and policy uncertainty.
    Working Paper VoxEU Summary
  2. "From Russia with Love: International Risk-sharing, Sanctions, and Firm Investments." (2024).
    Economics Letters with Kiet Duong, Toan Huynh, and Anh Phan.
    Summary: We show how targeted international sanctions drive cross-border financial spillovers, detailing how interconnected firms use risk-sharing mechanisms to buffer geopolitical shocks.
    VoxEU Summary EuroPP
  3. "Housing Wealth Reallocation between Subprime and Prime Borrowers During Recessions." (2022).
    Macroeconomic Dynamics with Ayse Sapci.
    Summary: We find an asymmetric impact of economic downturns on mortgage markets by tracing how housing wealth shifts between prime and subprime categories.
    Online Appendix
  4. "Did the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act Help Counties Most Affected by the Great Recession?" (2021).
    Review of Economic Dynamics with Mario J. Crucini.
    Summary: We document the county-level efficacy of ARRA fiscal stimulus funding to determine if government intervention accurately reached the hardest-hit regional economies.
    Working Paper Replication Data
  5. "Electrification, Telecommunications, and the Finance-growth Nexus: Evidence from Firm-level Data." (2021).
    Energy Economics with Christopher A. Cotter and Peter L. Rousseau.
    Summary: We show that infrastructure development acts as a critical catalyst for macroeconomic growth, exploring how reliable electrification amplifies specific financial outcomes.
    Working Paper
  6. "Asymmetric Effects of Sectoral Shifts under Low and High Uncertainty." (2021).
    Economic Inquiry with Kimberly A. Berg.
    Summary: We find that a sectoral shift induces more depressed economic activity under high uncertainty than under low uncertainty, and that these effects are not driven solely by recessions. We develop a tractable two-sector DSGE model with stochastic volatility that qualitatively replicates these asymmetric dynamics.
    Working Paper
  7. "Price Flexibility and Output Volatility under Menu Costs." (2020).
    Macroeconomic Dynamics
    Summary: I document the nuanced relationship between the frequency of firms adjusting their prices and the associated resulting volatility of aggregate output using a state-dependent pricing framework.
    Working Paper
  8. "International Effects of Stock Market Dispersion." (2020).
    Southern Economic Journal with Jiayu Wu.
    Summary: We show empirically how dispersion in cross-sectional domestic equity returns acts as a potent transmission channel for driving interrelated international business cycles.
    Working Paper
  9. "International Effects of Corporate Tax Cuts on Income Distribution." (2020).
    Review of International Economics with Siraj G. Bawa.
    Summary: We find cross-country evidence that corporate tax cuts widen income inequality both within and across borders through differentiated equity holdings. We illustrate this using a multi-country DSGE model calibrated to U.S. and Canadian data, showing that lower capital mobility barriers—but not lower trade costs—can alleviate the distributional divergence.
    Working Paper
  10. "International Spillovers of U.S. Financial Volatility." (2019).
    Journal of International Money and Finance with Kimberly A. Berg.
    Summary: We document exogenous shocks to the U.S. financial system and quantify their subsequent direct spillover effects on global macroeconomic volatility patterns and capital flows.
    Working Paper
  11. "Stock Market Volatility and International Business Cycle Dynamics: Evidence from OECD Economies." (2015).
    Journal of International Money and Finance
    Summary: I find robust empirical linkages between localized equity market fluctuations and global cycle synchronization using long-term historical data across OECD member nations.
    Working Paper

Working Papers

  • "State-dependent Distribution Friction and Monetary Policy." (R&R) with Dong (Carl) Cheng.
    Summary: We find that state-dependent distribution frictions weaken the real effects of monetary policy by dampening the consumption response to monetary shocks, using a multi-sector general equilibrium model with counter-cyclical distribution margins consistent with micro price evidence.
  • "Natural Disasters and Asset Prices: Evidence from Winter Storm Uri." (Under Review) with Dong (Carl) Cheng.
    Summary: We find that firms with greater ex-ante employment exposure to Winter Storm Uri experienced significantly larger abnormal return declines, particularly those with high investment adjustment costs.
  • "Real Effects of Inflation Expectation Uncertainty." (R&R)
    Summary: We find that inflation expectation uncertainty is negatively related to household consumption using NY Fed survey data. A dynamic model with cognitive discounting of future inflation shows that precautionary saving motives, higher markups, and expected real interest rate distortions jointly amplify the consumption decline.
  • "A Tale of Infrequent but Long-lasting Liquidity Trap." with Kevin X.D. Huang.
    Summary: We show that both the reduction in liquidity trap duration and the size of the government spending multiplier are hump-shaped in the magnitude of the fiscal stimulus, using a nonlinear DSGE model with heterogeneous firm productivity.
  • "Growth in a Time of (Projected) Debt." with Jackie McCafferty and Jonathan Wolff.
    Summary: We document a non-linear impact of public debt projections on economic growth. Utilizing a threshold regression model, we find that an increase in projected debt declines growth above a threshold, but increases growth below it. We show that these dynamics arise mechanically in an endogenous regime-switching DSGE model where forward-looking agents reduce taxed activities as sovereign debt approaches its fiscal limit.
  • "Sectoral Shifts and Geopolitical Risks." with Kimberly A. Berg.
    Summary: We document a novel link between geopolitical risk, sectoral shifts, and economic performance across 17 countries. We show empirically that unexpected geopolitical shocks induce sectoral reallocation and output declines. Using a two-sector DSGE model, we demonstrate that when this sectoral reallocation is subdued, the resulting output decline is correspondingly dampened.
  • "Labor Supply, Risk Aversion, and Conflict Uncertainty." with Kiet Duong.
    Summary: We show that an increase in conflict uncertainty negatively affects individuals' willingness to work, using proximity to the 2014 Ukrainian border as a plausibly exogenous proxy. Furthermore, we document that this response coincides with an increase in perceived risk aversion arising from the conflict, framing labor-supply fundamentally as a gamble.
  • "What Matters for the Rise in Trade? Evidence from the African Continental Free Trade Area." with Kiet Duong, Toan Huynh, and Povilas Lastaukas.
    Summary: We find that the AfCFTA increased firms' international sales-to-total sales ratio by 2.7 pp over five years, with gains concentrated among firms with prior overseas export experience, those in goods sectors, and those with lower capital adjustment costs.
    Slides
  • "Sanctions and Knowledge Spillovers." with Kiet Duong, Toan Huynh, and Steven Ongena.
    Summary: We find that international sanctions subject Russian patent applications to longer processing times and fewer forward citations in sanctioning countries. Interestingly, we also document that applicants with Kremlin-linked or popular Russian names, as well as those with recent filing history, systematically bypass these delays.
  • "Firm-specific Tax Incentives under Sanctions: Evidence from Russian Tax Authorities." with Kiet Duong and Toan Huynh.
    Summary: We show that Russian firms receiving tax incentives prior to the 2014 sanctions exhibited higher subsequent revenue, profits, and capital expenditures than their non-incentivized peers. However, these benefits accrued predominantly to private firms, displayed no efficiency gains in the military sector, and ultimately crowded out investment by firms that did not receive the tax incentives.
